01

Mono is Courier New, and it’s reserved for code

Conflict
Brand style guide
Courier New is the mono face, and it is reserved for code. Nothing else uses it.
App system
System monospace at 10px uppercase is the entire metadata voice – every column header, every metric label, every realm label, every chip. Roughly 40% of the text in the admin UI.
"Courier New is a monospaced font reserved for code."

This is the most consequential discrepancy in the audit, and it cuts both ways. If the app honours the brand rule, the mono label voice disappears entirely and every label becomes small Bely – which is legible but loses the visual separation between data and labels about data that makes the metric band readable. If the app keeps mono labels but switches to Courier New, they’ll look like code, because Courier is a much wider, more mechanical face than the system monospace I’ve been designing with.

My view: the app is a data tool and needs a metadata voice that Bely can’t provide. I’d propose extending the brand guide rather than the app – adding a documented "UI metadata" role for a neutral monospace, distinct from Courier-for-code. That’s a change to the brand system, so it’s your call. I’ve left --font-mono on the system stack that every screen in this project was designed against, and parked the brand face in --font-mono-brand, referenced by nothing. Point one at the other when you’ve decided – nothing else needs to change.

Decision needed
Extend the brand guide with a UI-metadata mono role, or drop mono labels from the app and rebuild the label voice in Bely?

06

Amber and olive are score levels, not chart colour

Fixed
"Amber … is used as the level 2 colour in the Content Health Check, halfway between fire and sunshine." · "Olive … is used as the level 4 colour in the Content Health Check, halfway between sapling and sunshine."

This is the real find, and it’s bigger than the missing lichen. My system described amber and olive as "chart colour only" – but they’re two rungs of the Content Health Check’s five-level score ramp, which is the core semantic scale of the entire product. It was missing from the design system altogether.

The two "halfway between" notes in the guide let the whole ramp be reconstructed: fire, amber, sunshine, olive, sapling. Now --level-1 through --level-5.

Worse than the omission: I’d used olive-500 as the third composition-bar segment, and as a health bar in the clients list. Any olive bar in this product now implies "level 4" whether you mean it or not. --comp-3 is reassigned to sorbet, and the level ramp is marked reserved.

Also worth doing
The Results and Estate screens presumably already use this ramp. When you send those screenshots I’ll check the app matches it, since this is the one scale a user actually learns to read.

09

Line-height and font-size scales were incomplete

Fixed

The brand guide defines a five-step line-height scale (tight 1 · snug 1.2 · normal 1.4 · relaxed 1.7 · loose 1.8) and --font-size-xs / --font-size-sm. This project had only normal and relaxed, and had normal at 1.5 rather than 1.4. All six now match the guide.

Note the guide’s --body-line-height is 1.4 where this project had 1.55. I’ve left the app’s body line-height alone – see divergence 12.

Deliberate divergences

Places the app system departs from the brand system on purpose. Worth recording so they don’t read as mistakes – but tell me if you disagree with any.

10

Textures are excluded from the app

Deliberate
"Combined with our textures it’s bold and organic, suggestive of craft rather than mechanisation."

The brand has a texture system (--texture-watercolour and friends) and treats it as central to the identity. The app system says no texture, no grain, no gradient.

Why: texture behind dense tabular data costs legibility for no gain, and this app is almost entirely tables and numbers. The brand character carries through the type and the palette instead. I’d revisit this for empty states, onboarding and the marketing-facing parts of the app, where texture would land well and there’s nothing to read through.

11

Base font size 17px, not 24px

Deliberate

The website sets --base-font-size: 24px with a responsive --text-multiplier. The app uses 17px with a fixed multiplier of 1.

Why: 24px body is right for a marketing site where reading is the task. In an admin tool with five columns and a metric band, it would halve the information on screen. The app’s own minimum floors still hold – 10px mono, 13px hint, 14px UI.

12

Bold used for emphasis in documentation

Deliberate
"We use bold, italic and underlining very sparingly indeed."

These audit and system pages use bold heavily to make rules scannable, and the do/don’t lists use sentence fragments rather than the full sentences with punctuation the guide asks for.

Why: internal reference documentation is scanned, not read – someone looking up "what colour is a warn chip" needs to find it in two seconds. Product copy and anything user-facing follows the guide properly. If you’d rather the documentation matched too, it’s an easy change.
