Audit · maturitytool.com against design-system/ · 7 September 2026

Nine things to take, eight to give back.

Read the live page and juliushonnor/maturitytool at 60279b7. The site is clean because it made nine decisions the system has never written down, and it is drifting because it is pinned to @contentious/ui#semver:^0.2 and has been building on a token vocabulary two rounds behind. One thing on it is a measured defect the system fixed in Content Health Check on 4 August and this page has reintroduced exactly.

What the system should take

Every one of these is a marketing-surface decision. That is the shape of the gap: the system has a complete app vocabulary and, for a page like this, one feature-card pattern and a section wash. Item 15 was added after review – I had filed the two-tone bullets as drift, and they are a gap.

01

There is no marketing page pattern, and this is the third one

Gap

contentious.ltd has bands, the Content Health Check homepage has feature rows, and this page has seven section types in a settled order: hero, benefit grid, reversed pull-quote, two-column scope, pricing bands, numbered timeline, closing call to action. All three sites arrived at the same rhythm independently and none of it is recorded. guidelines/pattern-feature-cards.html documents one row of one of those sections.

The composition is the reusable part. Alternating two grounds, one reversed band roughly two thirds down, and a single accent-filled closing section is a page shape, and you said there may be more of these sites coming.

Recommendation
A new guidelines/pattern-marketing-page.html holding the section order, the alternation rule and the ground pair. Items 02 to 08 below are its contents.
02

The eyebrow, written out four times on one page

Gap

.type-accent-label, .included-card__eyebrow, .pricing-card__eyebrow and .timeline__week are the same six declarations under four names: --font-size-sm, 0.08em tracking, uppercase, --fire-600, medium weight, a bottom margin. Only the margin differs.

.type-accent-label  ·  fire-600  ·  0.08em  ·  margin-bottom 1.5rem
.included-card__eyebrow  ·  fire-600  ·  0.08em  ·  margin-bottom 0.5rem
.pricing-card__eyebrow  ·  fire-600  ·  0.08em  ·  margin-bottom 0.5rem
.timeline__week  ·  fire-600  ·  0.08em  ·  margin-bottom 0.25rem

The system holds this twice already and neither is reachable here. .c-label is the app metadata voice: mono, --t-label, --label-tracking at 0.09em, --label-color. .c-feature__accent is the same declarations again, scoped inside a feature card, added on 4 August so the two FeatureCards could fold.

There is a real decision under this, not just a rename. The site's eyebrow is fire-600 in the heading face; the system's is grey in mono. Fire-600 measures 5.56:1 on the hero ground, so it is legal, and a coloured kicker is doing marketing work that a grey metadata label does not do. I would keep the site's colour and the system's face, and give the marketing eyebrow one class of its own rather than a third copy.

Decision needed
Is the marketing eyebrow a coloured variant of .c-label, or its own component? And does it keep fire, which would make the accent do a fifth job after primary, star-1, danger and link?
03

Pricing bands, and the featured card is not legal here

Gap

Three bands, one marked "Most common", each with an eyebrow, a title, a description, a figures block and a feature list. The system has nothing for this, and it is the section most likely to recur on a partner site.

The featured treatment does three things the system has settled against: a 2px solid var(--fire-500) border where .c-card--selected is the only sanctioned fire outline, a fire-tinted box-shadow, and transform: translateY(-4px). The 4 August feature-card decision is explicit: no lift and no shadow, because the app has neither and a shadow is a second surface depth.

The need is real, so this wants an answer rather than a ban. The system already has a card that means "different kind of thing from the cards around it": --surface-card-deep, with the constraint that it is never used twice in one set. A row of three where the middle one is deep says the same thing without a lift or a shadow, and the eyebrow already carries the words.

Recommendation
.c-price and .c-price-row, with --featured taking data-surface="deep". Also settles what the figures block is: it is currently lichen-300 inside a limestone-200 card, which is item 12.
04

A pull quote on a reversed band

Gap

The best section on the page. A gloaming band, a sorbet eyebrow, a 2em italic quote in two columns with running prose beside it, and an 8rem Bely Display opening quote mark bled off the top left. [data-surface="inverse"] gives the ground and every text role inside it; nothing in the system gives the editorial object.

Two details worth keeping and one worth fixing. The oversized display glyph is the only place in the suite where type is used as ornament, and it works. The asymmetric padding-top: 4rem on the prose column, so the second column starts at the quote's first line rather than at the top of the grid, is the kind of thing that gets lost in a rebuild. The glyph is --fire-400 at opacity: 0.6, which is the 1 August rule against opacity as a colour, in a decoration.

Recommendation
.c-pullquote, one per page, on an inverse band. The glyph takes a declared stop rather than an alpha, and the rule says it is ornament so it carries no contrast floor.
05

Numbered process steps, and the system has half an answer

Gap

The timeline is a three-across card grid, each card carrying a 2.5rem fire disc with a numeral in it, a week label, a title and a detail line. The system has --marker-node and .c-bullets--timeline, which are a node sitting on a line, read as a point in a sequence.

These are two different objects and both are right. A vertical rail suits a changelog; six steps with a week each do not fit a rail on a marketing page and are better as a grid. But the disc is 40px of solid accent repeated six times, which is more fire than anything else on the page, and the numeral in it fails contrast (item 13).

Decision needed
Does .c-bullets--timeline grow a grid variant, or is a numbered step grid its own component? I lean to its own, because the marker means "step 3 of 6" rather than "a point on a line" and takes a size the bullet scale does not hold.
06

The tapered hairline

Gap

The hero closes with a 1px ::after that is a horizontal gradient from transparent, through gloaming-900 at 10%, back to transparent. It is a section divider that is strongest in the middle of the screen and absent at both edges.

This is the same reasoning --wash-section is built on, applied to a rule instead of a fill: the 135deg angle is there so a washed section meeting a flat one divides at one edge and merges at the other, "and that taper is the point". The system has three hairline tokens and all three are constant-value lines. A tapered divider is the right weight between a full-bleed hard rule, which reads as chrome on a marketing page, and nothing at all.

Recommendation
Take it, as a section-level divider rather than a fourth --rule-* token: it is a gradient and the rule tokens are colours. Its alpha needs converting to a stop, same as item 04.
07

Marketing measure, in ch

Gap

max-width: 28ch on the h1, 48ch on the hero intro, 36rem on the closing subtitle, and a hard line break in the h1 via two spans. The system has --width-prose for app content and nothing for a marketing headline.

28ch on a title and 48ch on a lede are good numbers and they are the sort of thing that gets re-guessed on every new page. The manual span break in the h1 is the part I would not carry over: it fixes the break at one width and the heading is already constrained to 28ch.

Recommendation
Two measures in the marketing pattern page, and text-wrap: balance on the title instead of the spans.
08

Inline code in marketing prose

Gap

Two <code> spans in the pricing copy, styled as a fire tint at 10% with fire-700 text, plus a second treatment inside the accent section. There is no inline-code component in the system.

It also uses the wrong face by the 1 August decision: --font-mono is the system stack and is the app's metadata voice, and --font-mono-brand is Courier, which the brand guide reserves for code. A domain name in running prose is arguably neither, and that is worth deciding once rather than per site.

Decision needed
Is yourfirm.maturitytool.com in a sentence code, or is it a literal in the metadata voice? The answer sets the face.
15

The two-tone bullets, which I filed as drift and should not have

Gap

Item 11 dismissed .included-list as a hand-rolled marker system, which is true of the mechanism and misses the idea. The two scope cards use the same list with two dot colours: fire-500 under “Setup”, gloaming-400 under “Run the platform”. The colour is carrying one-off against ongoing, across two cards sitting side by side.

one-off  ·  3.89:1
ongoing  ·  4.42:1
.c-bullets today  ·  8.11:1

It passes the tests the system already has, which is why I would take it. The 5 August reservation says a 500 stop is free where the mark carries its own word, and both lists are under a heading, so neither dot can be read as a level. Colour means something, which is the standing requirement. Both stops clear the 3:1 floor for a non-text mark. And it answers a question .c-bullets has not been asked: the declared pair is filled against hollow, for done and not yet, which is a state within one list. This is a second axis, distinguishing which list you are in, and the system has no vocabulary for it.

Three cautions, none fatal. It does not scale past two, in the same way --comp-* stops at four, so the rule has to say two lists in one comparison rather than “a colour per list”. It makes fire the marker colour for half a list pair, which is the same fifth-job objection as the eyebrow in item 02, and the two should be answered together. And the halo is box-shadow: 0 0 0 4px at 18% alpha, which is the same alpha-as-colour problem as items 04 and 06, and is a different object from --marker-halo, which punches a gap in a timeline line rather than ringing a dot. The halo is also what makes the mark read as 18px of ink where --marker-size is 7.4px, so it is a size decision as well as a colour one.

Recommendation
.c-bullets--paired: two lists in one comparison, the primary taking the accent and the secondary taking --marker-color, capped at two. The ring becomes a declared stop and joins the --marker-* geometry rather than being an alpha in a shadow. Note the neutral dot here is gloaming-400 at 4.42 where --marker-color is gloaming-500 at 8.11, so adopting the token darkens it slightly, which I think is right on a marketing card.

What the site should take back

The site is pinned to ^0.2 and its CSS is written against the shadcn-era vocabulary. None of it is broken today, and all of it is a rebuild waiting to happen.

12

1.06:1, and it is the exact failure fixed on 4 August

Defect

.section-alt is --lichen-200. The cards on it are the Content Maturity theme's --card, limestone-200. That is 1.06:1, so on the two tinted sections a card has no edge except where its corners are, and the pricing figures block inside a card is lichen-300 at 1.14:1.

lichen-200 section
limestone-200 card · 1.06:1
lichen-300 figures · 1.14:1

This is the finding that moved the Content Health Check ground on 4 August, in the same words: a card sat 1.06:1 from the page while a hairline sat at 1.43:1, so the surface meant to be restrained separated seven times more strongly than the one meant to be definite. Three tokens existed only to route around it and were deleted when the page moved. Here the cards keep a border, so nothing looks wrong, which is why it survived: the outline is carrying the structure the fill is failing to.

Recommendation
A marketing ground pair for this product, authored, not two stops picked next to each other. Item 14 is the same problem one level up.

The structural one

14

Maturity Tool is a fifth product with no signature, and its grounds are 350 stops from the one it borrows

Structural

The page loads the Content Maturity theme and sets no data-product. Its own docs say "content-maturity theme: fire primary, lichen surfaces". So the type, the accent and the cards come from Content Maturity, and every ground on the page is picked by hand in styles.css:

hero               lichen-150
benefits, pricing   (unset, so the theme page ground)
scope, timeline     lichen-200
figures block        lichen-300
quote band           gloaming-550
closing                fire-600
footer                 gloaming-600

Content Maturity's --surface-page is lichen-500. The site's lightest section is lichen-150, which is 1.33:1 from it, and the two are not close to the same page. Three of the seven grounds also sit one or two stops from a token that already exists and means that thing: --surface-footer is gloaming-700, --surface-inverse is gloaming-800, and the quote band and the footer are gloaming-550 and gloaming-600, so four near neighbours are doing two jobs.

The site is not wrong to be lighter, and that is the finding. products.css already says deployment is a property of a surface rather than of a product, and that every reserved product has a working ground and a front door. What it does not have is a token for the front-door ground: the only marketing-specific ground the system holds is --wash-section, which is a two-stop wash authored per product, and Content Maturity's is lichen-350 to lichen-450. A page built on that wash would be much closer to this site than to the Content Maturity app, so the answer is nearly there and is not written where anyone would find it.

Decision needed, and it is the one that unblocks the rest
Is Maturity Tool a product with its own signature block, or a marketing skin on Content Maturity? If it is a product, the signature set grows a marketing ground pair and every product declares it. If it is a skin, the front-door ground belongs in the Content Maturity block and this site stops picking stops by hand.
