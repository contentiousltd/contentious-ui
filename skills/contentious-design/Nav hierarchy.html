You’ve found a proximity bug. The switcher sits at the far left and the four sections it governs sit at the far right, so nothing but convention connects them – and the moment a secondary strip appears, the switcher is touching that instead. Gestalt wins over intent every time: whatever is nearest is read as related.

A limestone-450 well encloses the switcher and its sections, with a hairline between the name and the pages. Containment is the strongest possible statement of "these four belong to this" – no ambiguity left.

The cost is a visible container in chrome that has to earn its ink on every page for a relationship most users infer in a day. It also makes the bar noticeably busier next to the wordmark. Worth it if you find people genuinely confused after Option 1 ships; otherwise it’s a solution one notch stronger than the problem.

The switcher and its four sections dim together when you’re in Account, Billing or Admin – because none of them is what you’re looking at. Dimming as a unit does two jobs at once: it says these five things are one group, and it says this group is not the page you’re on, the strip below is.

They stay clickable, at 42% – this is de-emphasis, not disabling. The group returns to full strength the moment you land on Estate, Inventory, Watchlist or Results. This is the piece that actually resolves the misreading in your screenshot, because the ambiguity is only ever present in an admin realm.

Rejected

Considered and not worth it.

NoSecond row
Give the project its own full-width row above the sections. Unambiguous, but it’s a third horizontal band of chrome that competes with the secondary strip for the same visual role – and on admin pages you’d have three stacked bars before any content.
Costs ~50px of every page to state something Option 1 states for free.
NoIndent the strip
Push the secondary strip’s contents right, so nothing sits directly under the switcher. Cheap, but it only moves the collision rather than explaining the hierarchy – and it breaks the left alignment the strip shares with page content.
Treats the symptom.
NoBreadcrumb the strip
Prefix the strip with the project: Sands › Account › Organisation. But an Account page isn’t inside a project – the account owns projects, not the reverse. It would state a hierarchy that doesn’t exist.
Actively wrong, not just redundant.

DoOption 1 + 3
Move the sections left to sit beside the switcher, and dim the whole cluster in admin realms. Together they cost nothing in chrome, state the hierarchy by grouping rather than by explanation, and remove the misreading in exactly the case where it occurs.
Two CSS changes and one conditional class. No IA change.

Keep the well in reserve. If Option 1 lands and people still hesitate, containment is the escalation – and it composes with the regrouping rather than replacing it.
