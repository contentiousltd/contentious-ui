Pattern · secondary navigation

One strip, everywhere.

Pick the horizontal strip as the only secondary navigation in the product and retire the side nav and the button-group tabs. Not because tabs are inherently better, but because three patterns for one job means users re-learn the page every time they land somewhere new – and it’s why Billing, Admin and Settings currently feel like three different products.

Level 1

Top bar – the work

Estate, Inventory, Watchlist, Results. Project-scoped, never changes. Plus the project switcher and the avatar menu, which are the doors into the admin realms.

Level 2

The strip – sections of a realm

Sits directly under the bar, same background, one hairline. Left: a mono label naming the realm you’re in. Right: the way out. Contents change per realm; the component never does.

Level 3

Doesn’t exist as navigation

If a section needs sub-views, use a segmented control inside the content (Monthly / Yearly, Last 7 days). If it needs its own page, it’s a level-2 tab. Never a second nav rail.

Anatomy

The strip carries the two things a side nav gives you for free and tab-buttons throw away: where am I, and how do I get back out.

Clients & projects

The h1 always repeats the active tab. Redundant on purpose – it anchors the page when you arrive from a deep link or a browser back button.

Realm label (mono, left)
Names the container the tabs belong to: Account, Project settings, Admin. Does the job the side nav’s heading was doing.
Exit (right)
Always leads back to the work – the current project. Admin realms are cul-de-sacs; give people the door.
Active marker
2px fire underline plus gloaming-800 text. No pills, no filled blocks, no boxes – the underline reads at a glance without competing with the top bar.
Order
Most-visited first, destructive last (Danger zone in fire, always the final tab). Never alphabetical.

Billing – side nav retired

Four items didn’t earn 280px of permanent rail, and the rail pushed the plan cards into a narrower column than they need. As tabs, the page gains its full width back and Billing stops looking like a different app.

The rules, short

Do

  • One strip per realm, directly under the top bar
  • Realm label left, exit right, always both
  • Repeat the active tab as the page h1
  • Segmented controls for sub-views inside a section
  • No strip at all when a realm has one section – the h1 carries it

Don’t

  • No vertical rails anywhere, at any level
  • No filled or boxed active tabs – underline only
  • Don’t put tabs inside a page body; the strip is chrome
  • Don’t exceed about seven tabs – split the realm instead
  • Don’t nest a second strip under the first
