COM front door

A starting page for contentoperatingmodel.com, built only from the kit and the token layer at data-product="com". Nav, hero, one body section, footer. It is deliberately short: sections get added as the content arrives, and the marketing kit in pattern-marketing-page.html is where the rest of them come from.

What is settled here and should not be re-decided per page. The data-product attribute on <body> or <html>, which is the whole theming integration. .c-marketing on every section, which re-derives the type scale to the 24px front-door density. The ground alternation, front and tint, and the section rhythm that comes with .c-marketing-section. And the eyebrow above a section title, which is the single strongest family cue on a Contentious page.

The artwork is real, and it is two cuts rather than one. images/com-apparatus.png is the lino-cut apparatus: transparent, 1200px square, the register in --signature-illustration rather than a stand-in for it. It carries the hero, the header mark at 40px and the footer mark at 29px, which is Julius's call and worth stating plainly because the small sizes are a compression: the drawing holds about thirty elements and at 40px they average four pixels each, so it reads as a texture and a silhouette rather than as a diagram of a machine. That is a legitimate thing for a mark to do. images/com-cog.png is the favicon: one shape – sunshine cog, gloaming hub, sapling centre – which is what survives 16px, where the apparatus cannot. Two cuts of one mark is already the house pattern: Content Health Check ships clipboard-gloaming.png and clipboard-pale.png for light and dark grounds. This pair splits on size rather than on ground. The diagram is separate again: published at contentious.ltd/resources/the-content-operating-model-in-the-ai-age in fire and sunshine, with the recolour still open in CHANGES.md.

Three things are flagged rather than solved, and the first one now has a number. .c-topbar is app chrome doing marketing duty: it holds a project switcher and an avatar in the app, and here it is brand, links and one action. It does not fit. The bar is nowrap because it was drawn at the app's 19px, and at the front door's 24px the brand alone is 347px, so brand plus four links plus the button overflow a 924px viewport by 140px and scroll the whole document sideways. This page patches it with flex-wrap in its own stylesheet, which is a patch and not a fix: a marketing nav variant is now a real requirement rather than a hypothetical one, and the decision it needs is what collapses first at narrow widths. The footer links, by contrast, are fixed rather than flagged: they were coral inside a sapling product until --accent-link-on-reverse landed, and they now read sapling-400 at 6.63:1 on the gloaming-700 footer ground. And the three-layer stack is page markup, not a component – three cards and a question grid, with the top layer on --surface-card-deep because the content layer is a different kind of thing from the two foundations under it. If it appears on a third page it earns a component; until then it is deliberately plain HTML so it is cheap to change.

The framework

The harness for your content function.

A Content Operating Model stacks three layers: an essence layer and a knowledge and opinion layer that steer, and a content layer on top of them that answers seven questions – why, what, where, when, for whom, by whom and how – encompassing humans, agents and the handoffs between them.

What it is

Where harness engineering names the scaffolding around an AI agent – the prompts, tools, hooks, feedback loops and recovery paths that turn a model into something that ships – a Content Operating Model names the same scaffolding at the scale of the whole function. It encompasses the work humans do alone, the work agents do alone, and the collaborations, handoffs and guardrails between them.

As your tools and team mature, the model stays stable. What flows through it shifts deliberately.
