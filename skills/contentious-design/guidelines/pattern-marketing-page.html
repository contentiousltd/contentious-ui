Read off maturitytool.com on 7 September 2026, which had answered every one of these in its own stylesheet. The specimen below is that page rebuilt on the kit, at data-product="mt". Workings: provenance/Maturity Tool site audit 2026-09-07.html.

This is a kit, not a template, and that is the first rule. Nothing here says a front-door page has a hero, then benefits, then a quote, then prices, then steps. Three sites in the suite arrived at roughly that order independently, which is why it is worth writing down, and it is a starting point rather than a shape to fill. Take the sections the page needs, in the order the argument needs them. What is NOT optional is the ground pair, the section rhythm, and the eyebrow: those are the things that make two different pages look like the same family.

A marketing page divides itself by alternating two grounds, and until 7 September the system had no token for either: the front door had a density, a motion token and a wash, and no page. So every marketing page in the suite picked its own stops, and two of the Maturity Tool site's put a card 1.06:1 from the section it sat on – the same number that moved the Content Health Check page on 4 August, hidden here because the cards carried a border and the outline was doing the work the fill was failing to.

The card is the whole test. Whatever the pair is, --surface-card must clear 1.18:1 from both of them, and a band step under about 1.15 does not read as a band. The pair cannot be derived from --surface-page: limestone's light end is aliased, so a recipe that reads on lichen is nearly invisible on Content Health Check. It is authored per product, like --wash-section, and the two are related here on purpose – lichen-350 is the first stop of Content Maturity's wash, and lichen-500 is its app page. So this product's marketing ground is a step lighter than the app it sells, and its band lands exactly on the app's own page colour.

A wash is not a band. --wash-section softens one section; the pair separates two. They compose, and a page that uses both should use the wash on the flush sections only.

Two things are deliberately absent. The accent-filled closing band has no component: it needs --text-on-accent to hold 4.5:1 on the accent fill, which is exactly why this product's accent is fire-600 rather than fire-500, and that is a small decision of its own rather than a component to ship on the way past. And .c-feature assumes an illustration and centres its text, so the shipping page's six text-only benefit cards have no answer here – either the page gains illustrations, which is what the specimen below shows, or .c-feature gains a text-only variant.
