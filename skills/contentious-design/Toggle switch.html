Component · toggle switch

Off is grey. On is fire.

The current switch fills the track with fire in both states, so the only difference between on and off is which side a 20px circle sits on – and nothing tells you which side means what. Filling the track is what a switch uses to say "active", so the off state has to give it up: a recessed neutral track, and the accent earned only when the thing is actually on.

Both read as "on". Glanced at out of context, neither state is legible – you have to read the sentence beside it to know what the control is doing.

Proposed – neutral off, accent on

Off is an empty recess: inset shadow, limestone-800, knob left. On fills with fire-600 and pushes the knob right. Readable with the label covered.

Why not sapling

The question is what the fill is claiming.

Fire – "this is live"

Matches what solid fire already means everywhere else – primary buttons, the active tab underline. The off switch makes no claim about whether off is a problem.

Sapling – "this is good"

Legible, but it borrows the colour that means good outcome in the data rules – so every off switch quietly implies something is wrong, and sapling stops meaning anything where it’s doing real work.

Do

  • Say the state, not the action: "Banner is hidden", never "Hide banner"
  • Emphasise the state word so it’s scannable without reading the sentence
  • Apply immediately, or fade the dependent fields until the switch is on
  • Keep the switch left of its label, 12px gap, one per row
  • Use a checkbox instead when the setting only takes effect on save

Don’t

  • Never colour the off track with the accent
  • Don’t use sapling/fire as on/off – this isn’t good vs bad, it’s active vs not
  • Don’t use a tint (fire-200) as the on fill – tints mean status, solids mean active
  • No "ON"/"OFF" text inside the track at this size
  • Don’t leave a disabled primary button looking like a pale accent button – grey it
  • Don’t stack a switch and its Save button with no visible relationship
